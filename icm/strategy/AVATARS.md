AVATAR 1 - UNAWARE: "Marcela, la viverista tradicional"
=====================================================================
Stage of Awareness: Unaware. She has never considered tillandsias as a product line and doesn't know she has a problem a new product line would solve. Her margins on common plants have been shrinking for years, but she experiences that as "la venta está floja," the weather of the business, not as a solvable problem with a name. She has seen air plants maybe twice, hanging in a cafe in Guadalajara, and filed them under "cosas de diseñadores," not "things I could sell." Nobody has ever pitched her a new species with a wholesale sheet, a story, and a live-arrival guarantee - her suppliers push the same twenty SKUs they push to everyone.

A - Who Are They:
Name: Marcela Fuentes. Gender: F. Job: owner-operator of a small vivero in Guadalajara, 12 years in business, one employee plus her husband on weekends. Household income: ~$45,000 MXN/mo. Marital status: married, two teenagers. Education: preparatoria plus agricultural technician courses taken at a community program years ago.

B - What They Do & Like:
Top 3 brands: Levi's, Nike, Viczza (local workwear). Hobbies: Sunday comida with the extended family, tending her own patio garden where she experiments with cuttings. Movies: Roma, Coco, Como agua para chocolate, Nosotros los Nobles, Instructions Not Included. Books: La vida es sueño, secondhand gardening manuals, Vive sin ansiedad, Crepusculo (borrowed from her daughter), El Principito. Websites: Facebook, MercadoLibre (as a buyer), Pinterest, YouTube, WhatsApp Business. Influencers: Plantornia, Deleites Botanicos, Vida Verde MX, Cosas de Viveros, a couple of agriculture YouTube channels she half-watches while repotting.

C - Why Are They:
Personality: practical, proud, cautious with anything new, slow to trust but extremely loyal once trust lands. Values: family above everything, honest work, the quality of her plants, keeping her word, staying debt-free. Victories: she survived 2020 without closing when three neighboring viveros didn't; her vivero is the reference point for her colonia - people give directions using her name. Failures: a 2019 orchid investment that rotted unsold on the tables and cost her a month of profit; a distributor in 2021 who shipped half-dead stock, promised a refund, and never answered again.

D - Smart Market Questions:
Awake at night: sales are flat and the big-box garden centers undercut her on everything common; she does the math of the month in her head instead of sleeping. Secretly afraid: the vivero slowly becomes "just another plant stand," that her kids see no future in it, that twelve years of work plateaus into decline. Angry at: Home Depot and Liverpool garden sections and their buying power; distributors who save their good stock for big accounts and treat small viveros as an afterthought. Top 3 daily frustrations: customers haggling over $20 pesos as if her plants were negotiable trinkets; supplier shipments arriving stressed or half-dead; the sinking sameness of her tables - nothing a customer can't find at four other viveros. Biggest secret desire: a signature product, something people drive across town to buy from HER. Built-in bias: she buys from people, not catalogs - a personal recommendation from someone she trusts beats any advertisement, and she distrusts anything that sounds like a pitch. Common language: "plantitas," "de a granel," "¿me lo dejas a cómo?," "que lleguen vivas," "a ver cómo se mueven." Top 3 complaints about existing solutions: everyone sells the same species; suppliers offer no story and no support; anything exotic carries minimums she can't risk.

E - Going Deep:
Top 3 dominant negative emotions: stagnation (the feeling of running to stay in place), quiet financial worry, resignation ("así es el negocio"). Top 3 positive emotions from solving this: pride in a distinctive table customers stop to photograph, curiosity and energy returning to her mornings, hope that the business can grow again instead of just survive. Beliefs: hard work deserves margin; plants are an honest business; you never bet the family's food on an unproven product. Biggest lifestyle desire: a vivero known for something special, modernized just enough, with her daughter running the Instagram and maybe, one day, the business.

F - Purchasing Habits:
Top 3 decision triggers: a supplier she trusts shows her something her competitors don't carry; the ability to test small (50 pieces is exactly her risk size); a live-arrival guarantee that removes the orchid-memory. Prior purchases for this pain: orchids (failed and scarred her), succulents (worked beautifully, then became commoditized and margin-less). Price tolerance: comfortable testing with $2,000-4,000 MXN; scales quickly if the tables empty in two or three weekends. Time horizon: she wants visible movement - plants selling, customers asking - within a month, or she mentally writes the line off.

G - Primary Wants:
Wants to gain: a distinctive, high-margin product line. Wants to be: the vivero known for "esas plantas de aire." Wants to do: offer something customers photograph and ask about. Wants to save: the business from commoditization. Wants to avoid: another dead-stock failure like the orchids.

H - Empathy Map:
Seeing: competitors with the same inventory she has; big-box prices she can't match. Thinking: "¿qué más puedo poner que no tenga todos?" Hearing: regulars asking "¿qué hay de nuevo?" Feeling: stuck, watchful, quietly worried. Saying: "ya todo lo venden en todos lados." Doing: reordering the same safe species, posting occasionally on Facebook, waiting for something to change.

How Tila reaches her: she will never find us by searching - she doesn't know the category exists. She arrives through the B2B pitch to viveros, led with: a product your competitors don't carry, cultivated in Mexico, minimum just 50 pieces, arrives alive guaranteed, wholesale sheet with photos. The first contact must feel like a person, not a campaign.

He's been to Etsy's retail pages and closed the tab at the prices. He is actively hunting; he just hasn't found the hunting ground.

Name: Diego Ramirez. Gender: M. Job: owner of a 4-person corporate gifting and event swag company in CDMX. Household income: ~$80,000 MXN/mo. Marital status: married, one baby. Education: licenciatura in marketing from a private university.

Influencers: Mexican marketing accounts, eco-design studios, Eventos Mexico pages, gifting-trend accounts, Simon Sinek clips that his team shares in the group chat.

Values: being the guy with the fresh idea, client loyalty earned through creativity, sustainability as a real practice and not just a pitch line, family time he's fighting to protect, his reputation. D - Smart Market Questions:
Awake at night: the Q4 corporate season - five proposals due the same week and every competitor pitching the identical catalog against him. Secretly afraid: becoming interchangeable, being seen as a vendor instead of a creative partner, clients taking his ideas and buying them cheaper elsewhere. Angry at: clients who squeeze his creative work and then choose on price; suppliers who slap "eco" on plastic. Biggest secret desire: the gift that makes a client's CEO stop and ask "who did this?" Built-in bias: he buys what he can photograph for the proposal deck - the story sells him first, the price second. Prior purchases for this pain: succulent kits (worked, now everyone has them), seed paper (fine, low wow), bamboo sets (the broken-shipment failure). Price tolerance: $40-90 MXN per unit on 100-500 piece orders if the story lands in the deck. Time horizon: needs quotes inside 24 hours and delivery inside two weeks of the event date - deadlines are sacred in his world.

G - Primary Wants:
Wants to gain: a catalog item competitors can't copy. Wants to be: the creative one. Wants to do: pitch something photogenic, sustainable, and genuinely Mexican. Wants to save: sourcing time and client relationships. Wants to avoid: another generic-catalog embarrassment in front of a director.

H - Empathy Map:
Seeing: the same ten products in every competitor's deck. Thinking: "necesito algo que nadie mas traiga." Hearing: clients saying "quiero algo eco, pero bonito." Feeling: hungry, slightly burned by suppliers. Saying: "mandame cotizacion y fotos." Doing: scrolling Pinterest at midnight, WhatsApping three suppliers with the same brief, comparing who answers first.

How Tila reaches him: this is the primary pitch target. Lead with the differentiator he can resell upstream: native Mexican air plants, cultivated at a named project in PV, min 50 pieces, wholesale sheet with real photos, live arrival.

She does not know a Mexican wholesale grower exists - let alone one in her own city. The gap between "what she knows works" and "what she thinks is available" is exactly where Tila lives.

A - Who Are They:
Name: Paola Villasenor. Gender: F. Job: destination wedding planner, Puerto Vallarta and Riviera Nayarit, 60-80 weddings a year, mostly US and Canadian couples. Household income: ~$120,000 MXN/mo in season. Marital status: engaged. Education: hospitality degree plus wedding-industry certifications.

B - What They Do & Like:

Books: The Wedding Book, Big Magic, wedding-design annuals, Eat Pray Love, El arte de la guerra (half-read, abandoned). Websites: Pinterest, Instagram, Etsy, Style Me Pretty, WhatsApp. Influencers: destination-wedding IG accounts, PV venue accounts, floral designers, Green Wedding Shoes, Junebug Weddings.

C - Why Are They:
Personality: warm under pressure, aesthetic-obsessed, a logistics machine wrapped in a smile, fiercely proud of her region. Values: her couples' day being flawless, local suppliers - "authentic Mexico" is literally her sales pitch to foreign couples - reliability above price, her vendor rolodex which is her real net worth, beauty with meaning. Victories: a 150-guest wedding featured on a major wedding blog; two venues now list her as preferred planner. Failures: a favor order from the US stuck in customs the week of a wedding - she paid triple for a local emergency replacement; a local artisan who ghosted her ten days before an event and still has her deposit.

D - Smart Market Questions:
Awake at night: a December wedding, 200 favors, and the Etsy shipping estimate says "maybe the 28th" and the wedding is the 20th. Secretly afraid: a supply failure on the day itself - her entire business is reputation and one viral bad review in a brides' Facebook group can cost a season. Angry at: international shipping timelines, customs roulette, vendors who overpromise with a smile. Top 3 daily frustrations: sourcing "authentic Mexican" details that aren't cliches; US-couple budgets that don't survive import costs; favors guests actually keep instead of leaving on the table. Biggest secret desire: a signature local detail that every one of her weddings carries - something living that guests plant at home and remember Puerto Vallarta by. Built-in bias: she buys local whenever local is reliable, and she will pay a premium for zero drama. Top 3 complaints about existing solutions: imported means slow and risky; retail prices murder the favor budget; local artisans who can't do volume or dates.

Beliefs: details are the wedding; local is the luxury her couples are actually paying for; a planner is only as good as her vendor list. Biggest lifestyle desire: fully booked two seasons ahead, known in the PV wedding circuit as the planner with the signature touch.

Prior purchases for this pain: Etsy air plants (arrived, painfully), local succulents (fine but unexciting), maracas and alebrijes (cliche risk). Price tolerance: $50-100 MXN per favor for 100-300 pieces, more if venue delivery is included. Time horizon: books three to six months out; needs the delivery date guaranteed in writing and a contact who answers on the wedding week.

G - Primary Wants:
Wants to gain: a reliable local favor source with real volume. Wants to be: the planner with the signature living detail. Wants to do: give guests a living memory of PV. Wants to save: budget versus imports, and her nerves. Wants to avoid: customs, dead plants, day-of disasters.
