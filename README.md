# Tila | Tillandsia bulbosa wholesale / Mayoreo

**International go-to-market plan and shipment controls. Reviewed 2026-09-30.**

Tila sells cultivated **Tillandsia bulbosa** air plants grown at Proyecto Indigo Azul, Puerto Vallarta. It is a for-profit wholesale business, separate from the seed company, Kupuri Media and New World Kids. Bulbosa is an epiphytic bromeliad, not a true bulb. This plan covers live ornamental plants for planting, not seed imports.

**Baseline:** Mexico first, minimum 50 plants, organic marketing only, no paid ads. Ivette leads Mexico; Bambú leads the US side. US expansion is a proposed, controlled wholesale pilot, not authorization to ship, spend or contact buyers. Other countries remain research-only until separately cleared.

**Legal status:** this README does not clear any shipment. The agency process is grounded below, but the exact Mexico-grown bulbosa commodity determination, current Mexican environmental status, nursery registration, importer, port, carrier and landed costs remain open. Obtain written agency/broker confirmation before accepting cross-border orders. This is operating research, not a legal opinion or an exhaustive review of every applicable law.

## 4. Pricing, cash and fulfillment

The old 42/60/85 MXN and US $2.50-3.50 examples are **not validated current prices**. Build dated quotes from actual costs:

`contribution = net sales - plants/cultivation - grading labor - packaging - certification/inspection/treatment - broker/customs - freight - clearance/forwarding - final delivery - payment/marketplace fees - unrecoverable tax - expected damages/returns`

`cost per saleable delivered plant = total shipment cost / saleable plants actually delivered`

Spread fixed export costs across the lot and account for damaged plants reducing saleable units. Show currency, quote expiry, freight quote and contingency assumptions. Suggested margin/damage thresholds are owner decisions, not established facts. Stress-test the quote for exchange-rate movement, inspection delay, partial loss and an unsuccessful shipment.

Negotiate a precisely named delivery term/place with the broker and buyer; prefer an experienced buyer acting as importer for the pilot. Do not quote DDP until importer authority, duties/taxes and responsibilities are resolved. Agree who pays treatment, delay, destruction/return and onward freight. A free sample still needs import compliance. Never label a commercial order as a gift or undervalue it.

Weigh/measure a packed 50-lot and test dry packing before selecting freight. Obtain written live-plant acceptance for the exact route; certification does not override carrier prohibitions. Use clean paper/carton packaging and measure arrival/acclimation quality. Avoid soil, bark, decorative moss and growing medium on the initial US pilot. Wood packing creates a separate ISPM-15 check. Do not invent transit guarantees.

## 5. Mexico -> US legal shipment workflow

### Identify and document the stock

Record **Tillandsia bulbosa**, live plants for planting, Mexico-grown, cultivated, count/grade, no soil/media, consignee and destination. Keep mother-stock acquisition/legal-origin documents, propagation records, nursery records and lot photos. Owning land or saying "we grow them" does not prove lawful original stock acquisition.

Have SEMARNAT/qualified counsel determine UMA/PIMVS registration and wildlife/forest controls, current NOM-059 status and scientific synonyms. The original 2010 list plus amendments is not a consolidated species clearance. **Bulbosa's current NOM-059 determination and nursery-specific status remain unresolved.** Non-CITES status is not an exemption from Mexican controls. [S7-S9]

### Establish US admissibility first

The importer checks **APHIS ACIR** for the exact taxon, Mexico origin, product form and destination, then obtains applicable **PPQ 587** conditions via eFile. APHIS says only US residents may apply; the owners' residency/importer authority has not been verified. Use a qualifying importer rather than assume eligibility. A 50-plant wholesale lot is not the 12-or-fewer exemption, and even exempt lots can have certificate/restriction requirements. [S1-S3]

**No definitive current ACIR commodity record or issued permit for this Tila route was obtained in this review.** Confirm NAPPRA/prohibitions, additional declarations, treatment, quarantine, destination rules and port routing from the actual determination. Save the result/permit version. No unrestricted-import claim is made.

### Mexico export certification

Provide the US conditions to **SENASICA** before packing. Its international phytosanitary certificate requires compliance with destination requirements, verification/inspection and the applicable payment/process. Arrange the authorized inspector/issuer and ask whether a SEMARNAT forest/wildlife lane also applies. APHIS does **not** issue Mexico's export certificate. Confirm current fee, validity, declarations and original/ePhyto handling, not a historical fixed amount. [S4,S8]

### Inspection, transport and customs

Follow the permit. If required for mail/courier, use the green/yellow **PPQ 508** label to the designated APHIS plant inspection station. Confirm the commercial route and labels with the station/broker. APHIS reviews documents and plants before release and can hold noncompliant shipments. Arrange importer contacts and onward freight before dispatch. [S1,S5]

The Mexican exporter/accountant and US importer/broker validate current tariff classification, Mexican export declaration/pedimento and exporter/RFC requirements, US entry, importer identification and bonds where applicable. SAT describes CFDI 4.0 with Comercio Exterior 2.0; applicability depends on the actual transaction. USMCA needs qualifying origin/support, not merely dispatch from Mexico; it does not waive phytosanitary rules or all fees. No duty/tax rate is quoted without current classification/entry review. [S10-S11]

### Documents and release gate

| Dossier item | Responsible party | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Species/lot identification and lawful cultivated-origin records | Mexico operation | Match actual stock/count |
| ACIR/agency determination; PPQ 587 if required | US importer | Mexico origin, product, destination, current conditions |
| Export phytosanitary certificate | Mexico exporter/authorized issuer | Declarations, dates, consignee, lot |
| CITES/SEMARNAT documents if applicable | Exporter/importer | Actual species/authorized quantities |
| Commercial invoice and packing list | Exporter | Scientific name, count, value/currency, seller/buyer, origin |
| Transport document and required APHIS labels | Carrier/importer | Inspection route/port and onward delivery |
| Fiscal/export and US entry records | Broker/accountant/importer | Classification, taxes and lawful declared value |
| Written carrier acceptance, inspection arrangements and claim terms | Owners/importer/carrier | Exact live-plant route and financial responsibility |

**Do not dispatch until all applicable items agree, costs/terms are approved and inspection/customs routing is confirmed. Do not redistribute before clearance.** Archive release and arrival QC. An invoice alone is not release.

## 6. CITES and restricted-species controls

The **full live CITES Appendices page** checked on 2026-09-30 states it is valid from **5 March 2026**. Bromeliaceae lists **Tillandsia harrisii, T. kammii and T. xerographica** in Appendix II, annotation #4. **T. bulbosa is not listed there.** The old seven-species list in the shipping notes is not current. [S6]

This is a narrow CITES finding, not unrestricted-trade approval. Recheck before shipment and whenever stock/taxon changes; inspect synonyms, hybrids/annotations and origin. Never extend bulbosa's finding to mixed lots. For regulated taxa, obtain applicable CITES documents and US protected-plant permits, including PPQ 621 where applicable. APHIS NAPPRA, federal noxious weeds, ESA and Mexican controls remain separate checks. No unidentified, wild-collected or pest-infested plants. The separate seed company needs its own species/safety review. [S2,S6-S9]

## 7. US -> Mexico, returns and other destinations

For any Tila sourcing, return or reexport from the US:

1. Query **SENASICA MCRFI** for species/product, use, origin and provenance. Save the exact sheet. If the combination is absent, request official requirements; no result is not permission. No specific bulbosa MCRFI combination was cleared here. [S12]
2. The US exporter works with an APHIS Authorized Certification Official, **PCIT** and **PExD**/official Mexican conditions. Complete inspection/testing/treatment and declarations before shipment within applicable time limits. Foreign-origin plants may need a **reexport** rather than export certificate. [S13-S14]
3. The Mexican importer completes applicable **VUCEM** documentary review, payment, **OISA** physical inspection and import certification, plus customs/environmental documents. An APHIS certificate alone is not Mexican release. [S15-S16]
4. Confirm importer/RFC, route, classification and carrier acceptance before booking. A rejected US lot cannot simply return without a return/reexport determination.

Canada/EU/other countries need separate species/origin-specific import, plant-health, environmental and customs review. Do not advertise worldwide shipping from US clearance.

Incluya cultivo, selección, empaque, certificación, inspección/tratamiento, aduana, transporte, liberación, entrega, comisiones, impuestos no recuperables y devoluciones. No ofrezca DDP sin resolver importador/impuestos. No declare compras como regalos ni subvalúe.
